# Community Evidence Review

A small research aid for evaluating whether one public online community may be a useful place to learn about a specific consumer problem.

## The Unique Kernel
Instead of giving an opaque match score, the review makes its reasoning inspectable and grounds evidence in source material the researcher can examine. It distinguishes five concepts: published rules are explicit statements in the community's published rules or description; direct source evidence is specific inspectable material relevant to the research problem; limited observations describe only what appears in the source material examined; AI inference is an interpretation of that material, not a fact; and unknowns are things the available material does not establish. The review shows what supports or weakens audience fit, while the researcher decides whether to investigate further or approach the community.

## Who It's For
An early-stage founder doing problem discovery for a consumer-facing idea. Today, they manually search for relevant communities, read their rules and recent discussions, and repeatedly reconsider whether the audience and outreach are a good fit.

## The Core Loop
For each new problem being researched, examine one candidate public community and decide whether it is worth investigating further. The founder returns to the process as they explore other problems or communities.

## Inspiration & Identity
No specific visual inspiration was established. The experience should feel neutral and evidence-led, with transparent reasoning rather than promotional language or an authoritative AI verdict.

## Why This Matters to the Learner
The learner wants to practice using a coding agent to build an AI-assisted, evidence-led proof of concept while understanding how the implementation works.

## What "Working" Looks Like
In a one-minute demo, a founder uses a fictional consumer problem and one public community to decide whether that community is worth investigating further. The result makes the specific problem under study and supporting or conflicting direct source evidence visible, with source material the founder can inspect; unsupported AI-generated claims are not presented as evidence. It separates published rules, limited observations, AI inference, and unknowns, explains the fit reasoning, and leaves the outreach decision to the founder. Limited observations describe only the source items examined. For example, "This issue appears in 3 of the 8 source items examined" does not establish that people in the community commonly experience it; claims about prevalence, representativeness, frequency, or the community as a whole require evidence and a sampling method that support them.

## The POC Boundary
Evaluate one public community in depth for one generic or fictional consumer problem. Evidence must be grounded in source material the researcher can inspect; the technical sourcing method will be decided later. Keep observations limited to the material examined, label AI interpretations as inference, and leave unsupported questions as unknowns. Do not claim prevalence, representativeness, frequency, or community-wide patterns unless the evidence and sampling method support those claims. Make the AI contribution clear by showing how it organizes evidence while preserving uncertainty. The founder inspects the evidence and makes the judgment.

## Later
Testing real outreach and learning whether it gets responses; evaluating additional communities; exploring the rest of the customer-discovery process.

## Explicitly Cut
- Ranking or comparing many communities: the proof of concept focuses on one community in depth.
- Lead generation, sales prospecting, and individual contact discovery: the user chose public communities, not individual leads.
- Deciding "post here" or "don't post here" on the founder's behalf: the founder retains the decision.
- A full customer-discovery or interview-management platform: this project demonstrates one small part of the process.
- Any use or disclosure of the learner's existing private MVPs, target customers, or future product plans: the example and project must remain generic and standalone.
